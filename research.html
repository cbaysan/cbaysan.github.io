Demand for Gender Equality in Conservative Contexts
With Carlos Molina, Antonia Paredes-Haz, and Gamze Zeki  ·  Draft available
Abstract

Many societies have experienced substantial sociocultural liberalization over the past century, yet isolating the role of elites in driving these shifts and the backlash they may provoke remains empirically difficult. Leveraging two independently implemented field experiments during Turkish elections, we estimate the causal effect of elite platform shifts toward more progressive positions on voter preferences. Pro-opposition parties advance distinct promises: one introduces a progressive stance on gender equality and women's rights, while the other focuses on conventional, non-sociocultural issues. Using official voting records, we find that gender-equality promises, rather than generating backlash, increase opposition vote share by 1.1 percentage points, a similar margin to the conventional campaign. Yet the campaigns reshape support within the opposition bloc differently. The gender campaign shifts votes toward the most progressive party, while the non-gender campaign shifts support toward the mainstream party, suggesting voters respond to issue salience rather than party persuasion. Using survey data, we uncover a striking divergence between administrative records and self-reported vote choice, especially pronounced for the gender intervention and accompanied by more regressive attitudes, which appear driven by misreporting. Our findings demonstrate that elite actors can activate genuine voter support for progressive policies in a conservative context, even when such policies appear to generate survey-reported backlash.
